What Counts as a Dental Emergency?

Know when to seek immediate care

😣

Severe Toothache

Intense, persistent pain that doesn't respond to over-the-counter painkillers may indicate infection or abscess.

High Urgency
🦷

Knocked-Out Tooth

A tooth that has been completely knocked out needs immediate attention. Time is critical for reimplantation.

Critical
💔

Cracked or Broken Tooth

Visible cracks, chips, or fractures that cause pain or expose the inner tooth require prompt treatment.

High Urgency
🩸

Uncontrollable Bleeding

Bleeding that doesn't stop after 15-20 minutes of applying pressure needs immediate dental attention.

Critical
😰

Dental Abscess

A pimple-like swelling on gums, facial swelling, fever, and severe pain indicate a potentially serious infection.

Critical
🔧

Lost Filling or Crown

While not always critical, a lost restoration can cause sensitivity and should be addressed soon.

Medium Urgency

What To Do Before You Reach Us

First aid tips for common dental emergencies

  1. Pick up the tooth by the crown (white part), not the root
  2. Gently rinse with water if dirty (don't scrub or remove tissue)
  3. Try to place the tooth back in the socket if possible
  4. If not, keep it moist in milk or saliva
  5. Seek emergency care within 30 minutes for best results

  2. Gently floss to remove any trapped food
  3. Apply a cold compress to the outside of your cheek
  4. Take over-the-counter pain medication if not allergic
  5. Avoid hot, cold, or sweet foods and drinks
